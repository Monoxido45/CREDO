**Adaptive $\gamma(x)$ ablation study:** Sensitivity analysis over $\gamma_{\min} \in \{0.05, 0.1\}$, $\gamma_{\max} \in \{0.5, 0.75, 0.9\}$, and $\tau_{\gamma} \in \{0.5, 1.0, 2.0\}$, evaluating their joint effect on SMIS, outlier coverage, and interval length ratio across 5 datasets. The analysis reveals a clear trade-off between metrics: more aggressive configurations tend to improve efficiency-oriented scores at the cost of epistemic uncertainty capture, and vice versa. The default setting ($\gamma_{\min}=0.1$, $\gamma_{\max}=0.75$, $\tau_{\gamma}=1$) provides a stable compromise across all datasets and metrics, and can serve as a principled starting point for practitioners wishing to adjust the balance according to their application needs.
